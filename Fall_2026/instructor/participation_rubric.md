# Participation rubric — weekly tutor transcripts

Participation is **50 of 100 points** — half the grade — spread across 12 weekly
tutor activities. It is the largest single component of the course, and right
now `course_overview.md` specifies it only as "full credit for engaging."

That is defensible pedagogically but not defensible if a student contests a
grade. This is a concrete rubric that keeps the low-stakes intent while giving
you something to point at.

---

## Design principles

1. **Effort, not correctness.** A student who reasons carefully to a wrong answer
   earns full credit. Say this out loud in Session 1 and mean it.
2. **Cheap to grade.** 12 weeks × ~20 students is 240 transcripts. Anything
   requiring careful reading will not survive October.
3. **Hard to game.** The failure mode is a student pasting "just tell me the
   answer" six times and calling it engagement.

---

## The rubric — 4 points per week, 12 weeks

Drop the two lowest scores; scale the remaining 10 to 50 points. Dropping two
removes every "I was sick" email from your semester.

| Pts | Looks like |
|:--:|---|
| **4** | Attempted a prediction *before* running code at least once. Answered the tutor's follow-up questions in their own words. Reached the wrap-up question. Wrong answers are fine at this level. |
| **3** | Engaged throughout but skipped the predict-then-run step, or stopped roughly two-thirds of the way through. |
| **2** | Short session. Answered some questions but mostly waited to be told, or ended early with no wrap-up. |
| **1** | Transcript exists but shows almost no reasoning — repeated requests for the answer, one-word replies. |
| **0** | No transcript, or a transcript that isn't from the assigned tutor. |

### The 30-second grading pass

Scan for three things, in this order:

1. **Does the student ever write a sentence longer than five words?** (separates 1 from 2+)
2. **Does the phrase "I think" or a prediction appear anywhere?** (separates 3 from 4)
3. **Did they reach the tutor's wrap-up question?** (separates 2 from 3)

That's it. If you're deliberating, give the higher score — the pedagogical goal
is that students engage without fear, and a rubric that makes them anxious about
being wrong defeats the whole design.

---

## Edge cases you will actually hit

**"The tutor wouldn't give me the answer and I got frustrated and quit."**
Full credit if the frustration is visible in the transcript alongside genuine
attempts. That is the tutor working as designed. Follow up in person — this
student is close to the lesson and needs one nudge.

**A student argues with the tutor and turns out to be right.**
Full credit, enthusiastically. Note it and use it as the recap example next week.
This is the single best outcome the course can produce.

**A suspiciously polished transcript — long, articulate, correct throughout.**
Possible the student ran the conversation through another model. Two tells: no
wrong turns anywhere, and answers that use vocabulary from later sessions. Don't
accuse. Ask them to walk you through one answer verbally in office hours. If
they can, they learned it, and the delivery mechanism doesn't matter much.

**The student used Claude/Gemini instead of the assigned ChatGPT tutor.**
Full credit if they pasted the same system prompt. The tutor is a prompt, not a
product — say so in Session 1 so this never becomes a question.

**Transcript is in a screenshot instead of text.**
Accept it this once, then remind them. Text is required because you need to be
able to search it; also, screenshots of long sessions are unreadable.

---

## What to tell students, verbatim, in Session 1

> Half your grade is showing up and wrestling with the weekly tutor. You get
> full credit for *engaging*, not for being right. I would genuinely rather read
> a transcript where you reasoned your way to a wrong answer than one where you
> guessed correctly. The one thing that loses points is asking the tutor for the
> answer six times and giving up — because that's the one thing that teaches you
> nothing.

---

## Optional: a mid-semester calibration

After Session 4, spend ten minutes reading four transcripts side by side and
check your scores are separating anything. If everyone is getting 4s, the rubric
is doing no work and you should either drop it or raise the bar for a 4. If a
lot of 2s are appearing, the tutors may be too rigid — check whether students
are hitting the "refuses three times" escalation rule and stalling out.
